CONCEPTS · CIRCUIT
06
The concepts · 01

The Racing Line

TAUGHT · INITIATION S2

The fastest way through a corner is rarely the shortest. Enter wide, clip the inside at the apex, run wide again on exit — straightening the corner to carry more speed.

  • Out — start at the outer edge to open the corner up
  • In — touch the apex, the latest point you meet the inside
  • Out — let the car run to the outer edge on exit
  • The dashed line hugs the inside — shorter, but slower: it forces a tighter, lower-speed arc
Racing line Slow / geometric line

The concepts · 02

Braking — Points & Threshold

TAUGHT · INITIATION S3

Brake hard, brake late, brake at the same place every lap. Pick a fixed reference — a board, a kerb — and hit the pedal there. Then brake as hard as the tyres allow, just short of locking.

  • Braking point — a fixed marker you can repeat lap after lap
  • Threshold braking — maximum deceleration right up to the edge of grip
  • Push past it and the wheels lock — you slide straight on, slower
  • The load-cell pedal teaches this by force, exactly like a real car
Speed Brake input
CONCEPTS · CIRCUIT

The concepts · 03

Trail Braking

TAUGHT · ADVANCED S1

Don't finish braking before you turn — overlap them. Keep bleeding off the brake as you add steering lock. The load stays on the front tyres, they keep biting, and the car rotates into the apex.

  • Brake and steering inputs overlap through the entry
  • Trailing brake pressure keeps weight on the front tyres for grip
  • The car rotates — pointing at the apex instead of running wide
  • The single biggest pace gain past beginner level
Brake pressure Steering angle

The concepts · 04

Throttle & Corner Exit

TAUGHT · ADVANCED S2

Slow in, fast out. Sacrifice a little entry speed for a later apex. It points the car straight earlier, so you can get on the throttle sooner — and that speed multiplies all the way down the next straight.

  • A later apex opens the exit and straightens the car
  • Get to full throttle earlier, feeding power to the grip limit
  • Exit speed carries down the entire straight — worth far more than entry
  • The dashed early-apex line looks fast on entry but runs wide and bogs the exit
Fast out (late apex) Slow out (early apex)
CONCEPTS · CIRCUIT

The concepts · 05

Understeer vs Oversteer

TAUGHT · ADVANCED S4

Every car has a balance — and you can read it, then change it. Understeer is the front giving up first; oversteer is the rear. Knowing which you have tells you how to drive it, and which setup knob to turn.

  • Understeer — nose pushes wide; ease off, or add front grip
  • Oversteer — rear slides; catch it with steering + throttle
  • Setup shifts the balance: tyre pressures, anti-roll bars, wing, diff
  • Advanced teaches you to dial out a problem — Tuner Lab makes you an engineer
CONCEPTS · RACECRAFT

The concepts · 06

Overtaking — The Switchback

TAUGHT · ADVANCED S5

The best pass often happens on the way out, not the way in. Sacrifice the entry, let the defender take the normal line, then cut back underneath for a far better drive onto the next straight.

  • 1 · Sacrifice entry — go in deeper and wider, off the normal line
  • 2 · Cut back — rotate behind the defender toward the apex
  • 3 · Better exit — superior drive lets you complete the pass cleanly
  • Also taught: the late-brake, the undercut, and racing etiquette — one move, leave space

CONCEPTS · RALLY
12
The concepts · 07 · rally

The Scandinavian Flick

TAUGHT · RALLY S2

On a loose surface, you steer with weight, not just the wheel. A quick flick away from the corner loads the outside tyres — then releasing it swings the car like a pendulum, rotating it into the turn before you've even reached it.

  • 1 · Flick away — a deliberate jab toward the outside
  • 2 · Weight transfers — load swings, the rear goes light
  • 3 · Rotate & power — the car pivots; you're already pointed at the exit
  • Pairs with trailing-throttle oversteer — lifting to rotate, not braking
CONCEPTS · RALLY

The concepts · 08 · rally

Pace Notes

TAUGHT · RALLY S4–S5

A rally driver races a road they've never seen — read to them, in code, by a co-driver. Pace notes describe every corner before it arrives: how sharp, how far, what's hiding over the crest.

  • Number — corner severity, 1 (hairpin) to 6 (flat-out)
  • Distance — metres to the next call ("100")
  • Links & modifiers — "into", "tightens", "opens", "don't cut", "crest"
  • The skill is rhythm and trust — the call lands exactly when you need it
CONCEPTS · ENGINEERING
14
The concepts · 09 · engineering

Weight Transfer & The Contact Patch

TAUGHT · TUNER LAB + EVERYWHERE

Grip follows load. Brake, and weight pitches onto the front tyres — their contact patch grows and they grip harder, while the rears go light. Every input you make moves weight, and weight is grip. This is the idea behind all of car control.

  • The contact patch — palm-sized — is all that connects car to road
  • Brake → load forward (front grip up). Accelerate → load rear. Turn → load outside
  • Trail braking and the Scandinavian flick are just weight transfer, used deliberately
  • Tuner Lab turns this instinct into setup: springs, dampers, bars, pressures
