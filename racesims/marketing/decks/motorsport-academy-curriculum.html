How we teach · the same shape, every session

The APEX loop.

Named for the point every corner is built around. One concept per session: we Aim at it, Practice it in isolation, eXecute it on a full lap, then eXamine the data together. Run identically by every coach, at every centre.

A · ~10 MIN
Aim
SET THE TARGET
Teach the one concept — never more than one. Show the BAD vs GOOD on a real telemetry example.
→
P · ~20 MIN
Practice
ISOLATED DRILL
The skill alone, stripped of everything else — braking with no steering, one corner repeated. Repetition builds the pattern.
→
X · ~25 MIN
eXecute
FULL LAP / STAGE
Return the skill to a full lap under realistic conditions. Make it hold up at speed.
→
X · ~5 MIN
eXamine
READ THE DATA
Coach + driver read the trace against the target, log the number, and set the next session's goal.

Progression is earned, never given — every tier is gated by a benchmark time logged on the driver's VMS Driver Card. Attendance passes nothing.

CONCEPTS · CIRCUIT
The concepts · 01

The Racing Line

TAUGHT · INITIATION S2

The fastest way through a corner is rarely the shortest. Enter wide, clip the inside at the apex, run wide again on exit — straightening the corner to carry more speed.

✗ BADTake the shortest path — hug the inside kerb the whole way through.
✓ GOODOut–in–out: wide entry, clip the apex, run wide on exit — the straightest, fastest arc.
Racing line Slow / geometric line
CONCEPTS · CIRCUIT
The concepts · 02

Braking — Points & Threshold

TAUGHT · INITIATION S3

Brake hard, brake late, brake at the same place every lap. Pick a fixed reference — a board, a kerb — and hit the pedal there. Then brake as hard as the tyres allow, just short of locking.

✗ BADBrake early and gently, coasting in — and braking in a different place each lap.
✓ GOODBrake late and hard to the threshold at a fixed marker — max grip, no lock-up, repeatable.
Speed Brake input
CONCEPTS · CIRCUIT
The concepts · 03

Trail Braking

TAUGHT · ADVANCED S1

Don't finish braking before you turn — overlap them. Keep bleeding off the brake as you add steering lock. The load stays on the front tyres, they keep biting, and the car rotates into the apex.

✗ BADFinish all braking in a straight line, then turn — the car won't rotate and runs wide.
✓ GOODBleed the brake as you add lock — weight stays on the front tyres and the car rotates to the apex.
Brake pressure Steering angle
CONCEPTS · CIRCUIT
The concepts · 04

Throttle & Corner Exit

TAUGHT · ADVANCED S2

Slow in, fast out. Sacrifice a little entry speed for a later apex. It points the car straight earlier, so you can get on the throttle sooner — and that speed multiplies all the way down the next straight.

✗ BADApex early and floor it — the car runs wide on exit and you have to lift.
✓ GOODSlow in, fast out: a later apex straightens the exit, so you get on power early and carry speed down the straight.
Fast out (late apex) Slow out (early apex)
CONCEPTS · CIRCUIT
The concepts · 05

Understeer vs Oversteer

TAUGHT · ADVANCED S4

Every car has a balance — and you can read it, then change it. Understeer is the front giving up first; oversteer is the rear. Knowing which you have tells you how to drive it, and which setup knob to turn.

✗ BADFight every slide with more steering and hope it sticks.
✓ GOODRead which end let go — front (understeer) or rear (oversteer) — then fix it with line, inputs, or setup.
CONCEPTS · RACECRAFT
The concepts · 06

Overtaking — The Switchback

TAUGHT · ADVANCED S5

The best pass often happens on the way out, not the way in. Sacrifice the entry, let the defender take the normal line, then cut back underneath for a far better drive onto the next straight.

✗ BADSend it down the inside and out-brake yourself — you both lose.
✓ GOODSacrifice the entry, cut back behind them, and pass on the better exit onto the straight.
Attacker Defender
CONCEPTS · RALLY
The concepts · 07 · rally

The Scandinavian Flick

TAUGHT · RALLY S2

On a loose surface, you steer with weight, not just the wheel. A quick flick away from the corner loads the outside tyres — then releasing it swings the car like a pendulum, rotating it into the turn before you've even reached it.

✗ BADJust yank the wheel into the corner on gravel — the car pushes straight on.
✓ GOODFlick away first, then back in — the weight transfer rotates the car into the corner for you.
CONCEPTS · RALLY
The concepts · 08 · rally

Pace Notes

TAUGHT · RALLY S4–S5

A rally driver races a road they've never seen — read to them, in code, by a co-driver. Pace notes describe every corner before it arrives: how sharp, how far, what's hiding over the crest.

✗ BADDrive only what you can see — always reacting late to the next corner.
✓ GOODDrive the note: the co-driver calls the corner — severity, distance, hazards — before you see it.
CONCEPTS · ENGINEERING
The concepts · 09 · engineering

Weight Transfer & The Contact Patch

TAUGHT · TUNER LAB + EVERYWHERE

Grip follows load. Brake, and weight pitches onto the front tyres — their contact patch grows and they grip harder, while the rears go light. Every input you make moves weight, and weight is grip. This is the idea behind all of car control.

✗ BADStab the brake or throttle mid-corner — the sudden load change unsettles the car.
✓ GOODMove weight smoothly — load the tyre you need to grip: brake loads the front, throttle the rear, turning the outside.
