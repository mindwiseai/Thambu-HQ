Session 1 · Cockpit & Car Control

The rig is not a game controller.

AIM
Concept: Weight transfer — the car has physical balance. Every input shifts it. Smooth inputs keep it settled; sudden ones unsettle it. The direct-drive wheel gives real feedback. The load-cell brake responds to force, not distance.
PRACTICE
Drill A — Braking Box: Brake in a straight line, stop within the box. No steering. Feel the load-cell pressure build.
Drill B — Smooth Slalom: Gentle steering inputs through cones. Goal: car never feels unsettled between gates.

10 laps of a simple circuit at 70% pace. Target is clean laps only — no spins. Speed is irrelevant in Session 1.

Review the input smoothness trace. Jagged steering/throttle vs smooth — the difference is visible. Coach logs the best clean-lap count.

INITIATION · CONCEPT · THE RACING LINE
Session 2 concept · before we drive

The fastest path through a corner is not the shortest.

TOP-DOWN: RIGHT-HAND CORNER · GEOMETRIC vs LATE APEX LINE

THREE PRINCIPLES

PRINCIPLE 01
Out – In – Out
Enter wide, aim for the apex (inside of the corner), exit wide. The larger the radius you create through the corner, the faster you can carry speed through it.
PRINCIPLE 02
Late Apex > Geometric Apex
Turn in later than instinct says. A late apex means you unwind the car early — maximising the straight speed after the corner. Early apex = runs wide on exit = scrubbed speed.
PRINCIPLE 03
Slow In, Fast Out
Sacrifice a little entry speed to carry maximum exit speed onto a straight. The straight is where you gain time; the corner entry is where beginners waste it chasing entry speed they don't need.
BAD
"I turned in early to hit the inside sooner" → runs wide on exit, tyres off the track, time lost on the straight.
GOOD
Wait. Turn in late. Clip the apex at the last moment. Unwind with power — straight-line exit speed is the reward.
INITIATION · SESSION 2 OF 4
Session 2 · The Racing Line

One corner. Perfect it. Then build.

AIM
Concept: The racing line — out–in–out, geometric vs late apex, slow in fast out. Coach shows the correct and incorrect lines on the whiteboard. Driver sees the speed trace difference before touching the wheel.
PRACTICE
Drill — The Apex Clip: Single corner on repeat. Hit the apex cone, unwind to wide exit. No lap, no pressure — just the one corner, repeated until the pattern is automatic. Coach calls apex miss or hit after every rep.
EXECUTE
Full lap, line-focused. Same corner type, now in context — other corners are at 70% pace. The session corner is where we look at the exit speed.
EXAMINE
Line overlay: the actual path vs the ideal. Coach shows exactly where the line broke down — too early, too late, too wide on exit. Logs the exit-speed delta.
SESSION 2 TARGET
Consistent line within benchmark + 4s · apex hit >80% of laps · no "runaway" exits

INITIATION · CONCEPT · BRAKING
Session 3 concept · before we drive

Where you brake is a decision. How you brake is a skill.

SPEED TRACE: BAD (late + lock) vs GOOD (threshold braking)

TWO THINGS TO MASTER

SKILL 01
Braking Points & Reference Markers
Pick a fixed reference — the 100m board, a kerb edge, a marshal post — and always brake there. Consistency before anything else. A variable braking point = variable lap times forever.
SKILL 02
Threshold Braking
Maximum deceleration just short of locking. The load-cell brake makes this teachable — you can feel the threshold through the pedal. Brake at the board, apply hard, then ramp off as you turn in. Not on/off — a pressure ramp.
BAD
Braking late to "go faster" — forces a lock or a panic lift. You lose more time in the corner than you saved on the approach.
BAD
On/off brake — stabbing and releasing. Unsettles the car at the moment it needs to be most planted.
GOOD
Brake at the marker. Squeeze hard, hold threshold, release smoothly as steering input begins. The car stays planted. The corner speed is higher.
INITIATION · SESSION 3 OF 4
Session 3 · Braking

Stop in the same place, every time.

AIM
Concept: Reference markers and threshold braking. Coach shows a speed trace from the previous session — highlights where time is being lost in the braking zone. Identifies the driver's current braking point and shows the benchmark.
PRACTICE
Drill A — Braking Box (scored): Brake at the marker, stop inside the box. Metric: brake-point consistency (±m) and zero lockups. Bronze/silver/gold targets set before the drill starts.
Drill B — Brake-and-Turn: Same drill into an actual corner entry — when does the brake release begin?
EXECUTE
Full lap with proper braking markers for every corner. Pace can come up — Session 3 is the first session where lap time starts to drop meaningfully.
EXAMINE
Brake trace + speed trace. Lockups, early/late braking points, release shape. The telemetry is the judge — not feel. Coach logs the best consistent braking point.
SESSION 3 TARGET
Zero lockups · repeatable braking point (±2m) · no "panic" early lifts

INITIATION · CONCEPT · VISION
Session 4 concept · the invisible skill

Your eyes lead. The car follows.

The fastest driver on any circuit is looking 1–2 seconds ahead — not at the kerb in front of them. Where your eyes go, your hands will follow. Most beginners drive by reacting to what's already at the nose. That's always too late.

BAD · SHORT VISION
Looking at the nose of the car
Driver reacts to what's already in front. Turn-in is late. Braking corrections are jerky. Every decision is a surprise. The car feels out of control — because the driver is always catching up.
GOOD · LONG VISION
Looking through the apex to the exit
By the time you reach the turn-in point, your eyes are already at the apex. By the apex, they're at the exit. The car feels smooth because your hands are responding to what you're about to reach, not what you're already at.

The coach will call "eyes up" during apply laps. The telemetry shows the effect immediately — smoother steering trace, better line consistency, higher corner speed.

INITIATION · THE DATA IS THE TEACHER
After every session · the debrief you actually use

You will see your improvement. Not just feel it.

Every session generates a telemetry trace. Coach and driver read four channels together. There is no "I think I was braking better" — the data confirms it or it doesn't.

CHANNEL 01
Speed Trace
Corner-by-corner speed — where time is won and lost. Minimum speed at each apex, maximum on each straight. If it went up, you improved.
CHANNEL 02
Brake Trace
Brake pressure over the zone. Is the point consistent? Is the pressure a ramp or a spike? Did you lock? The trace shows all three — nothing hides.
CHANNEL 03 + 04
Throttle + Line Overlay
Throttle application on corner exit — on/off vs ramp. Line overlay shows the actual path taken vs the ideal. Where the line diverges is exactly where you focus next session.

The coach doesn't say "you were a bit late on the brakes." The coach opens the trace and says "here, at Turn 3, your braking point moved 18 metres across these 10 laps — that's the inconsistency costing you 0.4s." That is what a programme feels like versus seat time.

INITIATION · BETWEEN SESSIONS
Between sessions · homework that actually trains you

Every session leaves you with one task.

You will leave each session with exactly one Practice Trainer to beat before the next. Not "work on your braking" — a specific scored exercise with a bronze/silver/gold target. This is how deliberate practice works.

AFTER SESSION 1
Drill: Smooth Slalom
Complete 5 slalom passes with no steering correction in between. Your steering trace should look like a sine wave — not a jagged spike. Target: 5 clean passes, input rate < threshold.
AFTER SESSION 2
Drill: The Apex Clip
The session corner, repeated alone. Hit the apex gate and exit wide: Bronze — 6/10 · Silver — 8/10 · Gold — 10/10 with exit speed > benchmark. Coach reviews your best attempt at Session 3 open.
AFTER SESSION 3
Drill: The Braking Box
Stop inside a shrinking box, zero lockups. Bronze — in the large box · Silver — small box, 0 locks · Gold — small box, 0 locks, consistent ±1m point across 5 reps.
HOW IT WORKS
The coached drill and the homework Practice Trainer are the same named exercise. Same setup, same metric, same bronze/silver/gold marks. Homework isn't something extra — it's the session drill you can now do unsupervised. The motor pattern builds across both.
WHAT THE COACH SEES
Your Practice Trainer results are on your VMS Driver Card. Session 2 opens with: "Silver on the Apex Clip — let's get you to Gold." The progression is visible, the next step is always named, and nothing resets.
